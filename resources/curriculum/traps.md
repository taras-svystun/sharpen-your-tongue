# Traps

The trap types shared by all schools (agreement 8 in `docs/PLAN.md`). A school's riddle names its type in that school's `reference.md`; the database tracks each type by its ID.

## How to read an entry

- **ID**: `trap:<name>`.
- **Mechanism**: what the trap takes from you, how it works step by step, and where it stops working (`resources/teaching-method.md` §1).
- **Post-soviet**: a riddle or concept from `resources/schools/post-soviet/reference.md`, by ID. The facts and quotes live there; here only what the example shows.
- **East coast**: an *illustration*, invented and written in plain English with no slang, because that school has no reference until step 9. It claims nothing about the school; step 9 replaces it with a sourced example.
- **Everyday**: the same trap at work or among friends.
- **Look-alike**: the trap or harmless situation it is most often confused with, and the test that tells them apart.
- **Answered by**: moves from `moves.md`, strongest first.

## trap:false-choice

**Mechanism.** You are offered a few options as if they were all there are, and every one of them costs you. The asker gains whichever you pick, and your own choice makes the loss look voluntary. It works because people answer the question they are given. It stops working the moment you notice the list is the asker's, not the world's.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:14`, a choice between two ways of being destroyed; the accepted answer refuses both halves.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Either you're with us or you're with them." He is with neither, and both labels cost him.
- **Everyday**: "Do you want to finish it tonight or over the weekend?", when the task was never agreed.
- **Look-alike**: a real dilemma, where the options truly are all there are. Test: can you name another option that only the asker's wording forbids? If yes, it is a trap.
- **Answered by**: `move:reject-frame`, `move:third-way`, `move:return-the-task`.

## trap:loaded-question

**Mechanism.** The question carries a premise, and any direct answer accepts it. "Yes" and "no" both lose because both stand on the hidden claim. It takes your consent to something that was never said aloud. It stops working once the premise is said aloud and refused.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:20`, «рога есть?»: either answer is mocked.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "You still running to the guards?" Yes or no, he admits he once did.
- **Everyday**: "Have you stopped missing deadlines?"
- **Look-alike**: `trap:false-choice`. Test: a false choice shows its options and hides the others; a loaded question shows nothing and hides the premise. Ask: what must be true for every answer to make sense?
- **Answered by**: `move:reject-frame`, `move:check-premise`.

## trap:misdirection

**Mechanism.** Attention is pointed at the wrong detail, while the answer sits in the words of the question or in a fact the frame keeps quiet. The trap takes your time and makes you look slow in front of others. It stops working when you read the question word by word before you answer.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:12`, the mug's name is the game's name; `ps:r:13`, the threatened option cannot happen at all.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Pay me back what you owe from last week." Nothing changed hands last week; the trap is in "last week".
- **Everyday**: a contract clause that sounds like a promise but only says "may".
- **Look-alike**: `trap:loaded-question`. Test: a loaded question wants your consent; misdirection wants your attention elsewhere. If the literal words already hold the answer, it is misdirection.
- **Answered by**: `move:check-premise`.

## trap:performance

**Mechanism.** An absurd or pointless task, set before an audience. Doing it makes you the show; refusing it badly makes you the sulky one. What it takes is your standing in front of the group. It stops working when you treat the task as the setter's problem, not yours.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:4`, play a broom like a balalaika; `ps:r:18`, shout at the window for a nickname.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Go ask the officer for the key to the yard." There is no such key; the block watches him go.
- **Everyday**: the new hire sent for "a left-handed stapler".
- **Look-alike**: `trap:nerve-test`. Test: does the task have a real risk the group itself has prepared for? Then it tests nerve, and refusing loses. If it only makes you look foolish, it is a performance, and doing it loses.
- **Answered by**: `move:return-the-task`, `move:decline`.

## trap:nerve-test

**Mechanism.** A frightening order whose danger is staged and controlled by the group. What is measured is whether you flinch, not whether you are clever. Refusing takes your standing; doing it calmly earns it. It stops being a test, and becomes simply danger, when nobody is in control of the risk.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:6`, jump head first from the upper bunk; the jumper is caught.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Walk across the yard alone. Let's see you do it." The others are watching his pace, not the yard.
- **Everyday**: being asked to present to the board with ten minutes' notice; the content matters less than staying steady.
- **Look-alike**: `trap:performance`. Same test as there, from the other side.
- **Answered by**: `move:hold-nerve`.

## trap:declare-yourself

**Mechanism.** You are asked to say who you are: your rank, your side, what you can do. Once said, every later act is judged against it, and a claim you cannot back is a debt. It takes your freedom to wait and be seen first. It stops working when you let actions speak before labels.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:24`, «кем будешь жить?», and the order to prove the claim at once.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "So you're tough, right? Say it." He says it, and now he must prove it to every newcomer.
- **Everyday**: "Are you a team player?" in an interview, before any example is asked for.
- **Look-alike**: `trap:fishing`. Test: who gains from your answer? If the person asking will hold you to it, it is declare-yourself; if someone else will hear it, it is fishing.
- **Answered by**: `move:decline`, `move:hold-nerve`.

## trap:fishing

**Mechanism.** A friendly question that collects information for someone with power over you. The asker is a courier; the real listener is absent. It takes the facts you would never give that listener directly. It stops working when you ask yourself where the answer goes next.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:1`, «кто ты по жизни?» in a pre-trial cell; see also `ps:t:sprashivat-interesovatsya`, why questions are suspect there.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): a new cellmate asks, kindly, who his friends are outside.
- **Everyday**: a colleague from another team asks, over coffee, what your manager really thinks of the project.
- **Look-alike**: honest small talk. Test: would the answer harm you if it reached the person with most power over you? Then treat the question as theirs.
- **Answered by**: `move:decline`.

## trap:unspoken-terms

**Mechanism.** You act before the price is said. Afterwards, the stronger side names the price, and witnesses agree with it. It takes whatever they decide it takes. It stops working when the terms are said aloud before the first move.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:2`, a game "for nothing" that turns out to have had a stake; see `ps:t:dolg`.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Take it, it's on me." A week later: "Remember what I did for you?"
- **Everyday**: "Can you just help us a little with this?", and the little becomes your project.
- **Look-alike**: a real gift. Test: could you refuse it now with no cost? A gift you cannot safely refuse is a debt.
- **Answered by**: `move:fix-terms`, `move:stay-out`.

## trap:drawn-in

**Mechanism.** One remark makes you a party to someone else's game, deal or dispute, and so a sharer of its loss. It takes your neutrality. It stops working when you say nothing about what is not yours.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:3`, «я бы пошел конем»: a hint at someone's game makes you liable for his loss.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): two men argue; he says "He's right." Now the other one has a quarrel with him too.
- **Everyday**: telling a friend which flat to buy; when the roof leaks, it is your fault.
- **Look-alike**: invited advice. Test: did a party ask you, and are you ready to share the loss? If not, keep out.
- **Answered by**: `move:stay-out`.

## trap:contact

**Mechanism.** Low status is treated as catching. Touching, sitting, eating with or taking from a marked person or thing marks you, whether you knew or not. It takes your standing at one stroke, and makes everyone afraid to help you after. It stops where the group holds that you cannot be judged for a rule nobody told you.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:7`, the only free chair is the unclean one; see `ps:t:mast`.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): the only open seat at the table belongs to the man everyone is shunning.
- **Everyday**: being the one seen lunching with the colleague the whole team blames.
- **Look-alike**: an honest warning about a rule. Test: was the rule told to you before you broke it? A warning before is help; a charge after is the trap.
- **Answered by**: `move:cite-their-rule`, `move:stay-out`.

## trap:admission-as-proof

**Mechanism.** You are asked a harmless question you answer truthfully, and the truth is then used as proof of a charge you cannot disprove. Your honesty does the opponent's work. It stops working when you never touch what is not yours, or never confirm a fact before you know what it will prove.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:8`, "Did you take the book? Then give back the hundred that was in it."
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "You were in the kitchen this morning, right?" "Yes." "Then where's my bag?"
- **Everyday**: "You opened the shared file yesterday?" "Yes." "So you deleted the sheet."
- **Look-alike**: `trap:loaded-question`. Test: the loaded question hides its premise in the words; here the question is clean, and the trap is in what the answer will be made to prove.
- **Answered by**: `move:stay-out`, `move:check-premise`.

## trap:silence-as-consent

**Mechanism.** An insult or a label is put on you in front of others. If you do not answer it yourself, at once, it becomes true: silence is read as agreement. It takes your status, quietly. It stops where the remark claims nothing about who you are: a joke or noise can be let go.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:9`, a man called unclean says nothing, and so accepts it; see `ps:t:otvechat-za-slova`.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "He's soft," said in front of the block, and he lets it pass.
- **Everyday**: in a meeting, "as usual, your part is late", unanswered.
- **Look-alike**: a provocation better ignored (`trap:performance`). Test: does the remark give you a place in the group? Then answer it. Does it only ask for a show? Then don't perform.
- **Answered by**: `move:answer-yourself`.

## trap:taken-at-word

**Mechanism.** A loose word of yours is read literally and held against you: a figure of speech becomes an accusation, a short year becomes a lie. It takes the benefit of the doubt that ordinary talk relies on. It stops working when you speak only words you would sign.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:11`, "you work for the guards" said as a figure of speech; `ps:r:25`, "85th" instead of the full year.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "You said you'd kill him." He said it as a joke; the man now waits for it.
- **Everyday**: "You said it would 'definitely' be done Friday."
- **Look-alike**: an honest misunderstanding. Test: does the other side gain from the literal reading? Then it is the trap, and explaining your meaning will not end it; taking care before speaking would have.
- **Answered by**: `move:check-premise`, `move:answer-yourself`.

## trap:loyalty-test

**Mechanism.** A story forces you to rank two loyalties: a friend against your standing, friends against your mother. Your choice shows what you hold sacred, and the wrong order marks you. It does not ask for wit but for knowing the group's order of values. It stops working when you know that order before you are asked.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:15`, a friend's snake bite against your standing; `ps:r:19`, the train.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Your boy talked to the police. You still stand with him?"
- **Everyday**: "Will you cover for me with the boss?" Your friend against your own word.
- **Look-alike**: `trap:false-choice`. Test: in a false choice every option is bad and you refuse the frame; in a loyalty test one option is right by the group's values, and you must pick it.
- **Answered by**: `move:name-the-principle`. Know the values first: the concepts in each school's reference.
