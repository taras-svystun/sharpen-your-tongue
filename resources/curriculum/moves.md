# Moves

The answer moves shared by all schools (agreement 8 in `docs/PLAN.md`). Which move answers which trap is written once, in `traps.md` ("Answered by"); the database tracks each move by its ID.

A good move keeps your position without rudeness, threats or leaving the conversation (`resources/teaching-method.md` §5). Fields are as in `traps.md`; **East coast** examples are invented illustrations until step 9, and **Look-alike** names the weaker answer most often mistaken for the move.

## move:reject-frame

**Mechanism.** Say that the question itself is wrong, and pick none of its options. A trap made of a frame dies when the frame is named, since its whole force was that you accepted it. It fails when the options really are all there are: then refusing is a dodge.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:14`, both halves refused, each with its own short answer.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "With you or with them?" "I'm with nobody. I do my time."
- **Everyday**: "Tonight or the weekend?" "Neither. We never agreed this task; let's see if it fits this week."
- **Look-alike**: `move:third-way`. Test: reject-frame refuses the menu; third-way adds a dish to it. If a third option exists that you are happy with, offer it; if not, refuse.

## move:third-way

**Mechanism.** Find the option the frame left out and take it. It keeps you in the conversation, so it never looks like a dodge. It fails when you invent a third way that is just one of the bad ones in disguise.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:16`, every sea has an island, every forest a clearing; `ps:r:10`, a complaint instead of either punishment.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Fight him or pay him." "I'll talk to him myself, tonight."
- **Everyday**: "Cut the scope or miss the date?" "Ship the core on the date, the rest a week later."
- **Look-alike**: `move:reject-frame` (see there).

## move:return-the-task

**Mechanism.** Hand the absurd task, or the rigged rule, back to the one who set it, in the same tone. The setter must now play his own game. It works because the trap needed you to be the only one on stage. It fails when the task is a real order from someone you cannot put on stage.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:4`, "tune the strings first"; `ps:r:5`, "the same as you".
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Go get the yard key." "You know where it is. Bring it, I'll wait."
- **Everyday**: "Can you fix the printer, you're the tech guy?" "Sure, as soon as you file the ticket I'll be in it."
- **Look-alike**: a mocking comeback. Test: return-the-task gives the setter a task; a comeback only scores a point, and invites the next round.

## move:check-premise

**Mechanism.** Before answering, test the question: is its hidden claim true, can its threat happen, what do its literal words say? Most traps of this kind fall to one plain question back. It fails when you check aloud too slowly and look unsure; check silently first.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:13`, there are no forks in a cell; `ps:r:17`, a КамАЗ has no bonnet.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Pay what you owe from last week." "What did I take last week?"
- **Everyday**: "Have you stopped missing deadlines?" "Which deadline did I miss?"
- **Look-alike**: arguing about the answer. Test: check-premise questions the question; arguing accepts it and fights inside it.

## move:decline

**Mechanism.** Give nothing, calmly, and say why not now. In the post-soviet school the stock form is «с какой целью интересуешься». It takes the courier's tool away without an insult. It fails when the question is fair and a refusal only makes you look like you hide something.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:1`, "we are all innocent here; who is who we will see later"; the stock phrase in `ps:t:sprashivat-interesovatsya`.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Who're your people outside?" "Why do you ask?"
- **Everyday**: "What does your manager really think?" "Ask her; she'd say it better than me."
- **Look-alike**: a dodge (walking off, going silent, changing the subject), which scores low by `teaching-method.md` §5. Test: decline stays in the conversation and gives a reason.

## move:answer-yourself

**Mechanism.** Answer a label put on you at once, yourself, briefly, and without an insult back. The label sticks only if it stands unanswered. Nobody else can answer it for you. It fails when the answer is louder than the insult: then you have performed.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:9`, the first word for yourself must be your own.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "He's soft." "Say that to me, not about me."
- **Everyday**: "As usual, your part is late." "It was due Friday; it's Wednesday. What do you need from it?"
- **Look-alike**: a counter-insult. Test: answer-yourself denies the label; a counter-insult accepts the fight and breaks your own standard.

## move:fix-terms

**Mechanism.** Say the terms aloud before the first move: stake, price, what happens if you lose. The trap of unspoken terms lives in the gap between act and price; closing it first leaves nothing to fill later. It fails when the other side will not agree terms: that is the answer, so do not play.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:2`, the lesson Kudin draws from the game "for nothing"; see `ps:t:dolg`.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "On me." "Thanks. What do I owe you for it?"
- **Everyday**: "Help us a little?" "Happy to give it two hours this week. Beyond that we talk to my lead."
- **Look-alike**: haggling afterwards. Test: fix-terms comes before the act; haggling after it is already the trap.

## move:cite-their-rule

**Mechanism.** Answer an order or a charge with a rule the other side itself must respect. They cannot reject it without breaking their own code in front of witnesses. It fails when you quote a rule you are not sure of: then you are the one taken at your word.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:7`, you cannot be judged for a rule you were not told; `ps:r:10`, the camp's own ban on approaching the strip.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "You sat in his seat." "Nobody told me it was his. Now I know."
- **Everyday**: "You broke the process." "The process doc says this step needs sign-off; I had it. Here."
- **Look-alike**: running to an outside power. Test: cite-their-rule uses the group's own code; calling in an outside power (the guards, HR) makes you depend on it, as `ps:r:3` warns.

## move:stay-out

**Mechanism.** Do not touch, comment on, or confirm what is not yours. Prevention: several traps need your first step, and this move never takes it. It fails when the matter is about you; then staying out is silence, and silence is consent.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:3`, never comment on another's game; `ps:r:8`, never take what is not yours, even to look.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): two men argue at the table; he finishes his meal and says nothing.
- **Everyday**: you keep out of a friend's flat purchase, or say "it's your call, I don't know the market".
- **Look-alike**: `move:answer-yourself`. Test: is the matter about you? Then answer; if not, stay out.

## move:hold-nerve

**Mechanism.** Do the staged frightening thing calmly, or stand still under pressure, without a show. The test measured your flinch, so not flinching passes it. It fails when the danger is real and nobody controls it; then it is not a test, and holding nerve means saying no.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:6`, the jump from the upper bunk; `ps:r:24`, hit the strongest and stay on your feet.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): he walks across the yard at his usual pace.
- **Everyday**: ten minutes' notice to present; you open with the one number that matters and keep your voice level.
- **Look-alike**: bravado. Test: hold-nerve is quiet and short; bravado talks about how unafraid it is.

## move:name-the-principle

**Mechanism.** When made to rank loyalties, answer with the principle that decides, not with a bare pick. The principle shows you know the group's values, and turns the choice from a confession into a rule. It fails when you name a principle the group does not hold.

- **Post-soviet**: `ps:r:15` and `ps:r:19`: «сегодня кент, а завтра мент» and the mother as sacred.
- **East coast** (*illustration*): "Your boy talked to the police. Still with him?" "Whoever talks is out. That's not about him, that's the rule."
- **Everyday**: "Cover for me with the boss?" "I won't lie for anyone. I'll help you fix it."
- **Look-alike**: a bare pick. Test: a bare pick answers this one case and invites the next test; a principle answers all of them.
